Home Price Estimator — Resources

About the Home Price Estimator

The Home Price Estimator is designed to be a simple, independent resource for exploring what a home price might look like based on two factors:

  • The monthly housing payment you feel comfortable with
  • The funds you have available for a down payment and closing costs

Using these inputs, along with estimated property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance (when applicable), and other assumptions, the estimator generates an illustrative home price example designed to align with both your budget and comfort level.

The estimator is intended solely as a resource for you. It is not designed to collect information about you, qualify or pre-qualify you for a mortgage, generate a sales lead, or steer you toward a particular lender, loan, property, or financial decision.

Its purpose is simply to provide relevant information and calculations that you can consider and use however you choose. What you do with the information is entirely up to you.

The goal is to provide a practical starting point for exploring homes within a payment range that you are comfortable considering.

How the Estimate Is Developed

The Home Price Estimator works from the user's available monthly housing payment and available funds for the purchase.

The estimator then considers estimated financing costs, property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance when applicable, and estimated closing costs to calculate an illustrative home price.

The result is intended to provide a home price example that considers both the monthly payment and the funds available to complete the purchase.

Data Sources & Modeling Assumptions

The Home Price Estimator uses published information, averages, assumptions, and mathematical calculations to develop its illustrative results.

  • Example Interest Rate (updated weekly) — Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey® (PMMS®). The survey reflects a market sample, not an actual rate.
  • Property Taxes — Tax Foundation, published property taxes by county; Effective Median Tax Rates for owner-occupied housing (2024). The Estimator applies tax rates to home price.
  • Homeowners Insurance — Bankrate, LLC., published homeowners insurance examples by state.
  • Condominium Insurance — NerdWallet, published condominium insurance averages by state.
  • Insurance Coverage Assumption — The Estimator assumes coverage of the greater of the loan amount or 80% of home price.
  • Private Mortgage Insurance — The Estimator uses illustrative PMI assumptions for down payments of less than 20%.
  • Closing Costs — Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac: usual/typical range of approximately 2%–5%. The Estimator uses 3.5%–5.5% of home price.
  • Property Type — The Estimator assumes a home is an owner-occupied, single-family, primary residence and is not in a flood zone, unless otherwise stated.

Data sources and modeling assumptions may be updated or changed over time.

Understanding Property Taxes

Property tax estimates are based on published effective property tax rates and are intended to provide a reasonable starting point for estimating the property tax portion of a potential monthly housing payment.

Property taxes can vary substantially between properties, even within the same county. A property's actual taxes may also be affected by assessment practices, exemptions, reassessment, purchase price, municipality, township, and school district.

For a specific property, users should verify current and projected property taxes with the appropriate local taxing authority or a licensed real estate professional.

Understanding Homeowners Insurance

The Home Price Estimator uses published insurance averages and modeling assumptions to estimate the homeowners insurance portion of a monthly housing payment.

Actual insurance premiums can vary based on the property, location, coverage, deductible, insurer, claims history, and other factors.

Association Fees

The Home Price Estimator does not include association fees in the estimated monthly housing payment.

If you are considering properties with association fees, such as condominiums, townhomes, or properties in a planned unit development, reduce the monthly payment amount entered into the estimator by the anticipated association fee.

Example: If your maximum comfortable monthly housing expense is $2,900 and typical association fees are approximately $400 per month, enter $2,500 as your comfortable monthly housing payment.

This adjustment helps account for association fees that may be part of your total monthly housing expense.

Flood Insurance

For simplicity, the Home Price Estimator assumes no flood insurance expense.

If a property is located within a designated Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA), flood insurance may be required by the lender and could increase the total monthly housing payment.

Because flood insurance requirements vary by property and location, the estimator does not attempt to estimate this cost.

Using the Estimate as a Starting Point

The Home Price Estimator is intended to help you establish a starting point for exploring homes based on a monthly payment and available funds that you provide.

As you explore homes online, properties priced above the estimated home price may potentially result in a monthly payment above the amount you indicated you were comfortable with, while properties priced below the estimate may fit more comfortably within your budget.

The estimate should be viewed as a starting point for your home search rather than a determination of what you can or cannot buy.
